## 1. The format in one breath

A 30-second, fully 3D-animated walkthrough of how a strange gadget would work, told in the second person ("you put on a headset…"). Every clause of the narration gets its own new camera angle. The video has no intro and no outro: it starts mid-procedure, explains the mechanism in three steps, then jumps the stakes to an absurd real-world use and ends on a comic anticlimax.

**Why it works**
- **Zero runway.** The first word is spoken at 0.0 s over an already-strange image: a gadget being lowered onto a turtle's shell. The premise is understood from the picture before the sentence ends.
- **Show what you say, literally and constantly.** Each spoken verb or noun appears on screen as it's said (straps appear on "strapped"; the headset goes on during "put on a headset"). A new angle arrives roughly every 1.3 s, and the camera is never still: 38–86% of the frame changes every 0.1 s.
- **Second person plus escalation plus anticlimax.** "You" (7.4 per 100 words) makes the viewer the operator. At 21.5 s (70% in) the stakes jump from a backyard to a war zone, and the last line undercuts it with the subject's own nature ("a slow getaway"). That joke is the payoff, so there's no need for a call to action.

## 5. Shot grammar

**Mix:** ~65% subject and gadget (close-ups, POV, wides) · ~25% the human operator (headset, electrode cap, over the shoulder) · ~10% diagram overlays. All CGI, with no talking head and no real footage.
**Rhythm:** a new angle on nearly every clause, about every 1.3 s. Cuts often hide inside fast camera moves; the detector flagged 17 of these as uncertain, and the sheets show different setups on either side of them. Speech covers 98% of the runtime, so the picture is the only thing that changes pace.

**Rules this editor follows** (inferred from the sheets):
- Illustrate every verb and noun literally, on the word (strapped → straps; "POV" → a POV shot; "look left" → the operator turns her head).
- Never hold a static frame: slow push-ins, focus pulls, orbits, low tracking shots at the subject's height.
- Make invisible processes visible with toy-like overlays: concentric red signal arcs, a white thought bubble with the imagined obstacle, glowing neon-green arrows for direction.
- Escalate locations, from a neutral studio (to explain) to a real place (to demonstrate) to an extreme place (for the joke).

## 6. Visual style guide

- **Look:** clean, semi-realistic 3D CGI with bright daylight, saturated colour, soft shadows, shallow depth of field and smooth constant motion. Palette:
  - studio cyan ~#3DB8E0
  - lawn green ~#5DA83A
  - operator magenta T-shirt ~#B23A82
  - gadget accent neon green ~#3CE36B
  - desert ochre ~#C9A06E
  - accents: red straps, blue electrode wires
- **Captions:**
  - Font: white bold geometric sans (closest free match: Montserrat Bold or Poppins SemiBold), sentence case, about 80 px on a 1080×1920 canvas (~4% of frame height).
  - Effects: soft drop shadow, no outline, no box, no highlight colour.
  - Position: centred at ~78% of the height, clear of the platform UI.
  - Timing: 2–4 words per card, switching on phrase boundaries in time with the voice.
- **Graphics and overlays:** built into the 3D scene rather than added as flat 2D graphics: red concentric signal arcs, a white cloud-shaped thought bubble holding the imagined object, glowing green and blue arrows. Each appears only while its line is spoken.
- **Characters:** generic, stylised-realistic 3D people (lab-coat hands, an operator in a bright T-shirt, soldiers). No real people.
- **Transitions:** hard cuts and fast camera moves that hide cuts. No dissolves (0 step changes measured).
- **Branding:** no watermark; the creator's name appears on a character's T-shirt at 0:27 as a subtle self-brand cameo (inferred).

## 7. Voice and delivery

**Measured:**
- **Pace:** 191 wpm overall, 210 while talking, steady at 180–216 per 5 s until the last line.
- **Pauses:** only 8 of 0.25 s or more, median 0.34 s, longest 0.42 s (before "When", the cause-and-effect beat).
- **Pitch:** median 128 Hz (low register), 88–225 Hz range, very animated, though the music bed inflates this figure (inferred). All 4 sentence endings rise.
- **Emphasised words:** device, put, camera, POV, think, forcing, sent, enemies, spying, through.

**Described:** an adult male narrator in a bright, quick documentary-meets-toy-commercial voice. Each sentence ends on a lift, so the story never "closes" until the punchline. Concrete nouns and verbs get the punch, and there are no breaths or filler. The tone is deadpan-curious: wild ideas, stated matter-of-factly.

**Voice direction** (for a narrator or a text-to-speech voice designer):
> Adult male, low-to-mid pitch, clean close-mic sound, no reverb. Read fast (about 190 words per minute) and bright, like an enthusiastic science explainer describing something cool but slightly absurd as if it's completely normal. Keep sentences flowing with tiny pauses (about a third of a second) only at commas. End every sentence with a slight upward lift so the story keeps rolling, then land the final phrase with a playful, deadpan drop. Punch the concrete nouns and verbs; never sound salesy.

## 10. Script template

Word budget ≈ 3.1 words per second (95 words in 30.5 s). Four sentences, all in the second person.

```text
[0:00–0:06 · SETUP · ~19 words]

[0:06–0:11 · MECHANISM 1: WHAT IT CAPTURES · ~18 words]
The {{SENSOR}} on {{SUBJECT}}'s {{PLACE}} {{CAPTURES WHAT}}, and its {{OUTPUT}} is {{DELIVERED}} {{TO YOU}}.

[0:11–0:21 · MECHANISM 2: CAUSE → EFFECT · ~28 words]
When you {{SIMPLE HUMAN ACTION}}, it {{SENDS A SIGNAL / TRIGGERS}} that {{CHANGES WHAT THE SUBJECT EXPERIENCES}}, making it think {{FALSE BELIEF}} and forcing it to {{DESIRED BEHAVIOUR}}.

[0:21–0:30 · ESCALATION + BUTTON · ~27 words]
Now, if you sent {{SUBJECT}} {{TO AN EXTREME SETTING}}, you could {{DRAMATIC USE}}, {{DOING THE COOL THING}} before {{ANTICLIMAX TIED TO THE SUBJECT'S NATURE}}.
```

## 13. Edit recipe

1. Record or generate the voice first. Trim pauses to about 0.3 s; the full read should be 29–31 s.
2. Split the script into clauses and give each one its own shot (aim for 18–22 setups in 30 s).
3. Build or generate the shots, keeping the camera moving in every one (push-in, orbit, focus pull or low tracking).
4. Cut on the clause boundaries, hiding some cuts inside fast camera moves. Change location at the start of beat 2 (studio → real place) and beat 4 (real place → extreme place).
5. Add the 3D overlays (signal arcs, thought bubble, arrows) exactly on their words.
6. Add the captions per the spec.
7. Lay the music under everything at −9 dB relative to the voice, master to −14 LUFS with a peak around −1 dBFS, and end on the last word.
8. Pick the escalation frame as the cover.
